        💡 Digital Safety Tips

        Simple daily habits that keep your phone, your money, and your personal details safer.

        📱 Phone & App Safety

        • Keep your phone's software updated - updates often fix security problems.
        • Set a screen lock (PIN, pattern, or fingerprint) so no one can use your phone if it's lost.
        • Only download apps from the official Play Store or App Store, never from links in messages.

        💳 Banking & Money Safety

        • Check your bank statement regularly for transactions you don't recognise.
        • Turn on SMS or app alerts for every transaction on your account.
        • Never make a payment because someone on a call is pressuring you to "act now."

        🌐 Internet & Social Media Safety

        • Be careful what personal details (birthday, address, travel plans) you post publicly.
        • Don't accept friend requests from people you don't know, even if they know your friends.
        • If a website looks unfamiliar or asks for too much information, close it and search for the official site yourself.

        📖 Know This Before You Trust Anyone Online

        Simple facts and questions to keep in mind - especially useful if you're still getting comfortable with phones, apps, or online banking.

        Quick Safety Facts

        • Banks, RBI, and government offices never ask for your OTP, PIN, or password - not on a call, SMS, WhatsApp, or email.
        • No genuine agency will threaten instant arrest or "block your account" over a phone call.
        • You cannot win a lottery or prize for a contest you never entered.
        • Real support staff never ask you to install a remote-access app on your phone or computer.
        • It is always okay to hang up, take your time, and call back on a number you trust.

        Cross-Questions To Ask Yourself

        Tap a question to see why it matters.

        Did I make this call, or did someone call me?

        If they called you out of the blue, be extra cautious. Hang up and call the official number yourself to confirm before doing anything.

        Am I being rushed to decide right now?

        Scammers create urgency on purpose so you don't have time to think. A genuine matter can always wait a few minutes while you check.

        Would my bank or office really ask for this over the phone?

        No real bank, government office, or company asks for your OTP, PIN, or password this way. If they do, it is a scam.

        Have I asked a trusted family member or friend first?

        A second opinion catches most scams. Never feel embarrassed to ask before you act - it takes one phone call.

        Can I check this myself, independently?

        Look up the official number or website yourself, instead of using the one given in the message or call.
